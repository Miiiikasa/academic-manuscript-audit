# References, citation mapping and verification

Separate **in-text/list correspondence**, **style**, **external metadata and
publication status**, and **support for the cited claim**. Passing one check
does not pass the others.

## In-text and list correspondence

Inspect body, captions, tables, appendices and the supplement as applicable.
Determine whether lists are shared or separate. Map author-year keys,
suffixes, numbered ranges and grouped citations to entries. Check missing
or unreferenced entries where applicable, ambiguous keys, duplicates,
first-citation order, and incorrect bibliography fields or stale display
text. Preserve exceptions the journal permits such as certain notes,
communications or dataset citations. Inspect the rendered markers if
superscripts, hyperlinks or field codes complicate extraction.

Apply the verified journal style and submission-stage exceptions, including
authors, initials, year, article title, journal title, volume, issue,
pages or article number, and DOI or access information. Treat preprints,
software, datasets, standards, policies and web sources as their own
reference types. Do not invent missing bibliographic fields or require DOI
for a source that does not have one. For reference-manager exports, inspect
rendered entries and their in-text links. If sampled, record coverage.

## External verification

Use official publication pages, persistent identifiers, trusted registration
metadata, versioned repository records or the original source where available.
Check identity and relevant metadata against the citation. DOI syntax and
resolution alone do not confirm a match. Compare electronic and issue dates
or versioned records before declaring an inconsistency. Search public titles
and identifiers, not unpublished surrounding prose or private data.

Look for relevant corrections, retractions or expressions of concern from
authoritative records when they matter. Describe how the work is used.
A reference to a retracted study can be valid when discussing the retraction
itself. An unresolved identifier or failed lookup is not proof of fabrication.
For a suspected mismatch, cite the specific conflicting fields and sources
and classify confidence and impact.

For key claims, inspect the relevant passage or actual evidence in the
source, and record whether access was limited to an abstract. Do not
generalise from a sample to every citation. Report counts and coverage for
citation matching, metadata verification and claim support separately.
Check key data and method source versions where they affect attribution or
reproducibility. Leave unverified sources explicitly open if access or
available evidence is insufficient.
