# Integrity, availability and author verification

Check each statement in three layers where applicable. Determine whether
the policy requires it, whether the supplied files contain it and agree, and
whether independent evidence or author confirmation supports it. One layer
does not establish the next.

## Data, code, materials and reproducibility

Match current journal requirements for data, code, materials, source data,
repositories, licenses, access and exemptions. Check that a statement reflects
the material used, actual versions, release status and restrictions. Review
repository landing pages, access conditions, release tags, dataset identifiers,
method steps, software versions, dependencies, parameters, seeds and
availability of analysis or figure inputs where relevant. A public landing
page or a statement that data are available does not establish complete
downloadability, lawful access or successful reproduction.

Look for clear links from key results to methods, source data and outputs.
Check filtering, missing-data handling, statistical assumptions and
computational environment when in scope. Separate documentation review
from tracing individual artifacts and reproducing results. Report what was
actually attempted. Actual replication may require a separately authorised
execution environment, access and time. Do not run arbitrary supplied code
or change any data or repository to complete an ordinary read-only audit.

## Disclosures and study-design requirements

Check applicable policies on ethics approval, informed consent, permits,
registrations, reporting guidelines, data agreements, funders, competing
interests, contribution roles, affiliations and author approval. Compare
statements across files and recorded facts with public registries or local
documentation when authorised and available. Some designs have documented
exemptions. Do not invent identifiers, infer exemptions, assign contribution
roles by rank or use a matching statement as proof of factual accuracy.

Use the current journal AI policy and distinguish writing assistance, research
use, coding, figures or media and other uses where the policy distinguishes
them. Record which use and evidence were reported. Check applicable
disclosures and authorship requirements, with author confirmation of actual
tools, purposes and use as needed. Check suspicious placeholders, inconsistent
method facts, questionable references and unverified provenance against the
evidence. Writing style does not establish that AI was used, and an internal
AI log is not automatically a journal submission requirement.

Check image and third-party material provenance, permission, data licenses
and processing when applicable. A screenshot or caption is not proof of
permission or of an unaltered source. Record visible observations and ask
for original files or documentation when necessary. In the absence of
evidence, classify as unverified and avoid an accusation.

## Items that may require the author

Flag actual authorship and contribution approval, authenticity of approvals
and consents, real conflicts or funding, AI use not otherwise documented,
permissions, access to non-public repositories, actual reproducibility,
portal-only inputs and final author sign-off. Some can be corroborated with
appropriate evidence or execution access. State the remaining action and
responsible party in the report. Record verified issues and unconfirmed
risks separately.
