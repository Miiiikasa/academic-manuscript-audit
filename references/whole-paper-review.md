# Whole-paper review decisions

## 1. Reverse outline and evidence map

Give each paragraph a stable locator: file + section + ordinal + opening words.
For DOCX, XML paragraph IDs can supplement these anchors; page numbers depend
on rendering and must not be invented. Include table/caption paragraphs in the
inventory but distinguish them from narrative paragraphs.

Map each narrative paragraph to its main function: background, gap/question,
design/definition, method procedure, finding, case/example, interpretation,
implication or limitation. Note the input it takes from the preceding paragraph,
the information it adds and the next question it prepares. Then test whether
that work requires its present length and location. In a
[follow-up audit](re-audit-and-revision.md), reuse verified maps and surface
only new, changed or still-open issues.
Paragraphs need not conform to a fixed rhetorical template.

Use this claim ledger for the main arguments:

| Question or promise | Method/definition | Result location | Interpretation | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|

A method name alone does not establish evidence for a claim. Check the actual
reported comparison, metric, time basis and population. Examples of invalid
inferences include a falling share interpreted as falling absolute output;
an endpoint comparison described as a monotonic trend; a cumulative statistic
used to establish temporal persistence; and a proxy treated as quality or capacity.
Context can sometimes supply the correct bounded reading: report a defect only
when that reading is not sufficiently clear.

## 2. Paragraph function and sequencing

Apply these tests in context:

- **Task:** Does the paragraph deliver the information its place and opening
  promise? A background paragraph that silently switches to the study's results
  may lose its job. An explicitly signalled end-of-Introduction findings preview
  may be legitimate for that journal/article type; do not ban it universally.
- **Centre:** Can the sentences be related to a defensible local purpose?
  Background-plus-gap, result-plus-bounded-interpretation and example-plus-
  explanation can be coherent combinations. Mere mixture is not an error.
- **Connection:** Is the inference real? “Therefore”, “because”, “nevertheless”
  and “together” must describe the relationship already supported by the text.
  Do not repair a missing mechanism with a causal connector.
- **Example:** State the exact proposition supported, what the case demonstrates,
  and its selection or scope where relevant. A repeated country or system may
  support different analytical dimensions. Proximity alone does not link a case
  to an argument; a fabricated generalisation is not a repair.
- **Order:** Are definitions and premises available when needed? Is the reader
  given enough evidence before a strong interpretation? Preserve settled order
  when it still serves the argument. Identify the specific reader cost and make
  any proposed move explicit.
- **Information gain:** Does a lead, transition or ending orient, narrow, compare,
  qualify or synthesise? Test whether nearby text already does the same work.
  Retain the smallest unit that carries the function readers actually need.

Useful topic sentences need not contain numbers. Useful synthesis need not
introduce new data. Do not require every paragraph to end with a takeaway.

## 3. Necessity and deletion test

For plausible candidates, take the sentence or whole paragraph out in a mental
copy and read the seam from the preceding passage to the following one. Check
the rest of the section and the paper for the deleted premise or callout. Ask
what is lost in evidence, orientation, comparison, qualification, transition or
interpretation, and whether a different passage already supplies it at the
moment of need. Apply this to correctly written text and previously retained
passages as well as obvious repeats.

- If omission costs nothing material, consider deletion.
- If one sentence or clause does the work, compress or merge and name its
  destination.
- If the full passage is useful elsewhere, compare its existing and proposed
  positions and trace any dependent citation or figure callout.
- If readers would lose a distinct inference, necessary setup, citation or
  independent reproducibility, keep the relevant material and explain the loss.

A past decision records a reason and a scope. Respect author instructions about
what may be changed. A newly demonstrated whole-paper cost can warrant a review
finding even when earlier local wording review passed. Identify that cost and
distinguish revisiting a placement decision from repeating a stylistic request.

## 4. Repetition and information allocation

Build an occurrence ledger for the main repeated numbers, case studies,
definitions and conclusions. A lexical/number search finds candidates, not
defects; compare the analytical object and denominator before grouping entries.

| Repeated item and scope | Locations and respective jobs | Best home for full detail | Alternate allocation and resulting loss/gain | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Choose between the current layout and a specific alternative even when each
appearance has a plausible local purpose. Compare full detail at the best
evidential location with short callbacks at points of interpretation. In
adjacent Results subsections check repeated ranks, shares and examples under
the same denominator before redistributing them. Across Introduction and
Discussion distinguish the setup or literature gap from the inference now
available from the study. Preserve recurrence when it saves a backward search,
supplies a valid comparison, or supports a stand-alone element.

Necessary recurrence includes:

- Abstract summarises a finding; Results supplies its evidence; Discussion
  explains its meaning, integration or limits.
- A result is recalled briefly to support interpretation without replaying all
  the figures. A useful reminder is not automatically redundant.
- The same case contrasts aggregate scale with portfolio composition or another
  distinct metric. Identical numbers can act as a controlled comparison.
- Methods gives the operational definition; SI repeats what readers need to
  reproduce the procedure; a caption defines a stand-alone figure's denominator.
- A limitation reuses a definition to restrict an interpretation.

Wasteful recurrence includes adjacent paraphrases with no additional function;
the same case used to establish the same proposition repeatedly; Discussion
replaying numerical Results without interpretation; and SI restating broad
definitions while omitting the procedural details its heading promises.

For a proposed deletion or move, specify the minimal retained occurrence, the
destination if any, and whether a definition, qualifier, citation or reference
must move with it. Do not mechanically choose “first mention wins”; the later
occurrence may be the one needed for a self-contained method or interpretation.

## 5. Argument quality, separate from correctness

| Test | Evidence to look for | Weakness worth reporting |
|---|---|---|
| Why the question matters | The Introduction identifies what the reader cannot infer from current knowledge and what answering this question adds | Importance of the broad topic is established but the specific question has only an inventory of gaps |
| Hierarchy in Results | Identify the primary finding, its best evidence and what each secondary result qualifies or adds | Several rankings or comparisons arrive at equal weight, hiding the result the section exists to establish |
| Case placement | Match each case to the exact claim it supports and put it where that claim first needs evidence | A case serves a local description yet interrupts the main comparison or repeats an inference already earned |
| Discussion synthesis | Show how findings together refine the question, change an interpretation, or delimit a generalisation | A paraphrase of Results occupies the place where readers expect to learn what the findings imply together |
| Contribution | Locate the specific question, unit, data, design or finding that differs from the closest prior study, and point to the result that demonstrates it | Generic novelty or importance language gives the reader no way to distinguish this paper from the closest work |

Check the manuscript's description against the nearest published primary
sources when available. Record what was inspected (abstract, relevant sections,
full article), the actual overlap and the supported difference. Compare shared
data and distinct analysis separately. Identical reported totals do not establish
identical records. Do not claim independent evidence, firstness, lack of prior
work or a new dataset without verification. Keep any unresolved overlap explicit
and use bounded wording. Use public citation identifiers for external searches
and keep unpublished manuscript text and data local.

Judge significance in proportion to design. A descriptive study can contribute
a consequential comparison or a distinction between measures. Use what the
results establish. A weak argument does not license a causal story, policy
implication, value judgement or additional scientific result.

## 6. Section expectations, adapted to paper type

| Section | Usual job | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Concise question, design, principal findings and bounded meaning | Unsupported novelty, overclaim, every result listed, empty closing frame |
| Introduction | Establish context, prior work, gap and research purpose | Catalogue with no gap, unearned value claim, unmarked switch to results |
| Methods | Define objects, units, procedures, measures and interpretation limits | Changed denominator, undefined scope, missing link between measure and question |
| Results | Report evidence and comparisons by result family | Conflating metrics, unsupported mechanism, cases with no identifiable purpose |
| Discussion | Interpret, integrate, position, qualify and delimit | Numerical replay, new unreported result, stronger scope than evidence |
| SI | Supply reproducible detail and supplementary evidence | Internal inconsistency, detail promised but absent, empty main-text replay |

These jobs do not impose IMRaD on reviews, theory papers or other article types.
Combined Results/Discussion may legitimately alternate evidence and interpretation.
Do not add a Conclusion, policy paragraph or new section to satisfy a template.
Where a Conclusion is present, trace its main claims to results already shown.
Flag unsupported new findings, implications or recommendations.

## 7. Independent editor and reader assessment

After the technical review, give a one-sentence takeaway grounded in the
results. Identify the passages that most directly earn it, the correct material
that advances it least, and the places where an editor could see a thin
contribution, loose progression or excess description. Tie each impression to
locations and observed reader effort. Separate this assessment from claims of
factual error or likelihood of acceptance. Use it to rank the smallest repairs
that make the paper's existing evidence easier to see.

## 8. Global change-impact check

Before recommending a repair direction, trace its effects on the affected passage and paper.

1. Same value/precision, denominator, unit, period, population, classification,
   direction, citation, hedge and operational meaning.
2. Same supported relationship; no new causal, normative or scientific claim.
3. Paragraph still performs its task and follows its neighbour coherently.
4. Later claims still have their premises; first definitions and pronouns survive.
5. No stranded citation, equation reference, figure/table callout or duplicate
   created elsewhere. Reference fields and files remain untouched in audit mode.
6. The proposed direction respects the terminology ledger and the section's register.

If an invariant would change, mark **待人工确认** and describe the scientific
decision needed. Record the direction and affected sites in the issue report.
Keep proposed prose internal.
